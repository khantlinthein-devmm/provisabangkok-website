Working legally in Thailand as an expatriate professional, manager, teacher, or corporate executive requires two separate legal authorizations: a valid Non-Immigrant Category B (Business) Visa issued by the Royal Thai Immigration Bureau and a Work Permit issued by the Department of Employment, Ministry of Labour.

This detailed guide outlines the legal criteria, corporate sponsorship obligations, the Digital Work Permit system, and continuous immigration compliance rules for businesses and foreign employees in Bangkok.

## The Dual Legal Framework: Visa vs Work Permit

A frequent point of confusion for foreign professionals is the distinction between a visa and a work permit:

- **Non-Immigrant B Visa:** Grants foreign nationals permission to enter and reside legally inside the Kingdom of Thailand for commercial or employment purposes. A visa on its own does not grant the right to perform work.
- **Work Permit (or Digital Work Permit D-WP):** Issued by the Department of Employment, this official document specifies the employee's approved job title, job description, workplace location, and sponsoring employer.

Performing any work—whether compensated or voluntary—without a valid work permit is a serious violation under the Emergency Decree on Non-Thais' Working Management B.E. 2560 (2017), subjecting the individual to fines up to 50,000 THB, deportation, and an immigration re-entry ban.

## Corporate Sponsorship Eligibility and Quota Rules

To sponsor a foreign employee for a Work Permit and 1-Year Non-B Extension, a Thai registered company must satisfy strict statutory thresholds:

### 1. Capitalization Requirement
The sponsoring entity must possess at least 2,000,000 THB of fully paid-up registered capital for each foreign employee hired (reduced to 1,000,000 THB if the foreign employee is legally married to a Thai citizen).

### 2. Thai Employee Quota
For standard private limited companies, the employer must employ at least 4 full-time Thai staff members registered in the Social Security Fund (SSF) system for every 1 foreign work permit holder. Companies approved by the Board of Investment (BOI) enjoy exemptions from this ratio.

### 3. Minimum Salary Scale by Nationality
Under Thai immigration revenue rules, foreign workers must be paid a statutory minimum monthly salary according to nationality:
- Western Europe, North America, Japan, Australia: 50,000 THB per month
- South Korea, Singapore, Taiwan: 45,000 THB per month
- Eastern Europe, South America, South Africa, Central America: 35,000 THB per month
- Philippines, India, Indonesia, China, Malaysia: 35,000 THB per month
- Other ASEAN and African countries: 25,000 to 30,000 THB per month

## Complete Document Checklist

### Documents Required from the Foreign Employee:
- Original passport valid for at least 12 months with valid Non-B entry stamp.
- Certified degree certificates, professional diplomas, and resume/CV detailing relevant work experience.
- Medical Certificate from a licensed Thai clinic or hospital (issued within 30 days) testing negative for Syphilis, Tuberculosis, Leprosy, Elephantiasis, and substance dependency.
- 4 passport photographs (3x4 cm or 4x6 cm, business attire, white background).
- Address verification (TM.30 receipt from Bangkok residence).

### Documents Required from the Sponsoring Employer:
- Company Affidavit (DBD Certificate) issued within 6 months, listing registered capital, directors, and authorized signatories.
- Shareholder List (BorJor.5) certified by the Ministry of Commerce.
- VAT Registration Certificate (P.P.20) and recent monthly VAT returns (P.P.30).
- Corporate Income Tax return (P.N.D.50) and audited financial balance sheet for the preceding fiscal year.
- Social Security filing forms (SorPorSor 1-10) with proof of bank payment for the preceding 3 months.
- Personal income tax withholding filings (P.N.D.1) showing employee compensation.
- Workplace photos showing the company building, front signage, and foreign employee interacting with Thai colleagues in the office.

## Step-by-Step Procedure to Obtain Non-B & Work Permit

### Step 1: Secure Initial 90-Day Non-Immigrant B Visa
Submit the company sponsorship dossier to the Royal Thai Embassy or Thai E-Visa portal in the employee's country of departure. Alternatively, if the candidate entered Thailand on a 60-day tourist entry stamp with at least 15 business days remaining, an in-country conversion to Non-B can be executed at Chaengwattana Immigration Division 1.

### Step 2: Digital Work Permit (D-WP) Application
Upon arrival or visa conversion, the employer files Form WP.1 with the Department of Employment. Under Bangkok's modern paperless initiative, foreign employees receive a Digital Work Permit credential accessible via the official Thailand D-WP smartphone application.

### Step 3: 1-Year Non-B Extension of Stay
With the active work permit issued, the employee and corporate legal representative attend Immigration Division 1 to extend the temporary stay permit into a full 1-Year Extension of Stay based on business employment.

## Ongoing Immigration Obligations: 90-Day Reporting & Re-Entry

### 1. Mandatory 90-Day Address Notification (TM.47)
All foreign nationals residing in Thailand for more than 90 consecutive days must report their current address to the Immigration Bureau every 90 days. This reporting can be performed:
- Online via the official Immigration Bureau e-Service portal up to 15 days before the due date.
- In person or via an authorized proxy at the Immigration Service Center (Chamchuri Square or Chaengwattana).
- Failure to report on time carries a statutory fine of 2,000 to 5,000 THB.

### 2. Re-Entry Permit Protection
Whenever leaving Thailand for overseas business travel, ensure you obtain a Single (1,000 THB) or Multiple (3,800 THB) Re-Entry Permit. Departing without an active re-entry permit automatically terminates your 1-year extension stamp and invalidates your work permit.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Can I work from home or in a co-working space in Bangkok on a Non-B visa?
Your work permit authorizes employment specifically for the registered company and address listed in your Department of Employment profile. If your employer adopts hybrid or remote operations, ensure secondary office locations or branch offices are formally registered on your permit.

### What happens to my visa if I resign or my employment is terminated?
A Non-B extension of stay tied to employment becomes invalid on the official last day of employment. The sponsoring employer must issue a formal termination letter, and the employee must visit Immigration to cancel the extension stamp before exiting the country or switching to another visa category.

### Can a foreign business owner sponsor their own work permit?
Yes, foreign directors possessing signing authority in a Thai company can sponsor their own work permit, provided the company meets the 2,000,000 THB paid-up capital and 4 Thai employee requirements.
